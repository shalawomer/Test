What actually happens to your file

  1. 1
    Rumble removed. A high-pass filter clears handling thumps, AC rumble, and mains hum below 80 Hz.
  2. 2
    Noise profiled and subtracted. An FFT denoiser tracks the noise floor across the whole recording and subtracts it frame by frame, so fans, hiss, and street noise drop away while speech stays.
  3. 3
    Residue cleaned. A non-local-means pass removes the broadband grit the first stage leaves behind, without the underwater artifacts of aggressive gating.
  4. 4
    Voice shaped. De-essing tames harsh s sounds and gentle compression evens out quiet and loud passages.
  5. 5
    Levelled and returned. Loudness is normalized to −16 LUFS, the podcast standard, then peak-limited. Video keeps its original picture untouched — only the audio track is replaced.

FAQ

Does my file leave my machine?

It goes to the Clearwave server you are running and nowhere else. No third-party API is called. Both the upload and the result are deleted automatically an hour after processing, and the New file button deletes them immediately.

What comes back?

Audio uploads return a 48 kHz 16-bit WAV, so nothing is lost to a second round of lossy compression. Video uploads return an MP4 with the original video stream copied bit-for-bit and a fresh 192 kbps AAC audio track.

Which setting should I use?

Balanced handles most recordings. Use Light for music or anything where the noise is mild and you care about the ambience. Use Strong for loud, constant noise — a fan, a car, a busy café — accepting that heavy reduction can thin out the voice.

Does it work on music?

Yes, though the chain is tuned for speech. On music, choose Light and turn off loudness normalization to leave the dynamics alone.
